Plan the request

Deciding what each photo or video of the problem should show

Your request will be read before anyone sees your home, so each image has to answer a question the reader would otherwise have to ask. A wide shot may show where the problem sits, a close shot may show a label, and a short video may capture a sound or movement. For your Sudbury home, take each one from where you can stand safely and add a one-line caption.

  • Wide shot for location

    Stand back far enough to show the fixture or equipment in its room, so the reader can tell where the problem sits.

  • Close shot of labels

    Photograph any rating plate, model label or tag square on and in good light, and caption it with what the label belongs to.

  • Video for sound or movement

    Record a short clip where a noise, drip or float movement matters, and say in the caption what you were doing at the time.

Before you send

What to Photograph Before You Send a Request

Clear photos help a provider understand the job before anyone visits. Keep them handy for the follow-up call or email.

  • Water heater: the rating plate, the vent pipe where it leaves the tank, and the rental sticker or contract if there is one.
  • Blocked drain: the fixtures that drain slowly or back up, whether it's only the lowest level, and the floor drain or cleanout if it's accessible.
  • Leak: the wet area or stain, the water meter with all fixtures turned off, and any recent work near the leak.
  • Sump pump: the pit with the lid off, where the discharge pipe leaves the house, and any battery or water-powered backup.
  • Older supply pipe: the line beside the water meter. Copper is orange-brown, galvanized steel is grey with threaded joints, and lead is dull grey and shows bright silver when scratched lightly with a key.
FAQ

Questions About Your Sudbury Request

How many photos should I include with a plumbing request for my Sudbury home?

Enough to answer the main questions: one wide shot for location, one close shot of any label, one of the symptom and one of the connections around it. Add a short video only where sound or movement matters. More images help only when each carries a caption saying what it shows.

What should a one-line photo caption say in a Sudbury plumbing request?

Say what the image shows and what question it answers, for example the water heater label, taken square on, or the drain video showing the gurgle when the sink empties. Add which fixtures were running if that matters. Keep it to one sentence. A caption saves the reader from guessing what to look at.

Should I open a cover or panel to photograph a Sudbury plumbing problem?

No. Photograph only what you can see from where you can stand safely, and say in the caption what you could not reach. Opening covers, panels, cleanout caps or pits is left to the provider, who may ask for access later. Do not force a valve that will not turn, and stop using a fixture that backs up.

Once my photos are sent, has a provider been found for my Sudbury address?

No. Coverage, availability and price all remain unconfirmed when a request is sent. Our enquiry team reviews the request first, and a provider must then confirm the address, the work type, qualifications, written scope, pricing and scheduling before anything is agreed. Until then nothing has been booked, and the images are only a description.

How do I describe an odour or noise that a photo cannot show?

Write it in the caption or the request: where it is strongest, when it starts and which fixtures were in use. A short video can carry a sound, but an odour needs words. If you smell gas near the heater, leave and contact the gas supplier or emergency services from outside.

Can one Sudbury request include photos of a stain and a slow drain?

Yes, if the symptoms may be connected, such as a ceiling stain below a bathroom and a slow drain nearby. Label each image with the symptom and the room, so the reader can separate them. Unrelated items, such as a dripping tap and a softener question, are clearer as separate requests, each with its own images.

What can I photograph when the pipes behind my walls are hidden from view?

Photograph the visible ends of the problem: where pipe enters the home, shutoffs, the meter, and the stained or damp surface itself. Caption where the pipe disappears, rather than guessing what lies behind. The provider decides whether openings, non-invasive checks or other evidence are needed, and a written scope should say so before work is agreed.
